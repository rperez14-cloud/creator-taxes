Sound familiar?

Creator income is easy to earn and easy to get wrong at tax time.

A pile of 1099s

A 1099-NEC from one brand, a 1099-K from a payment app, a 1099-MISC from a platform, and some income with no form at all. All of it still has to be reported.

The surprise tax bill

No one withholds taxes from creator income. On top of income tax, self-employment tax runs about 15.3% of your net profit.

"Free" products

Gifted products and trips you receive in exchange for content are generally taxable income at fair market value, even though no cash changed hands.

Missed deductions

Cameras, lighting, editing software, a home studio, props, travel for shoots. Many creators pay more than they need to because nothing was tracked.

FAQ

Questions creators ask us.

I only made a little money. Do I still have to report it?

All income is reportable, whether or not you receive a 1099. If your net self-employment earnings are $400 or more, you generally owe self-employment tax and need to file.

Are gifted products really taxable?

Products or services you receive in exchange for content or promotion are generally treated as income at their fair market value. We'll help you figure out what applies to you.

Do I need an LLC?

Not necessarily. An LLC can offer liability protection, but it doesn't change your taxes by itself. We'll walk through whether it, or an S-corp election, makes sense at your income level.

I'm behind on taxes. Can you still help?

Yes. We help creators file prior-year returns and respond to IRS notices.
