GAO: Self-Employed Workers Leave $80 Billion in Taxes Unpaid Each Year

Before the April 15, 2027 tax deadline, Creator Tax Pro shares 5 simple steps YouTubers, TikTokers, and influencers can take to report their income the right way.

From Creator Tax Pro · Ruben Perez, Tax Advisor | AFSP
November 11, 2026

NEW YORK, NY, November 11, 2026 — The deadline to file and pay 2026 taxes is April 15, 2027. A report from the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) shows that people who work for themselves often report less income than they really earn. Creator Tax Pro, a bilingual tax service for content creators, wants to help creators get their income right before that deadline, without fear or confusion.

The GAO is the government's watchdog. In its report, Sole Proprietor Compliance (GAO-24-105281), it looked at sole proprietors. These are people who run a business on their own, without forming a company. Most YouTubers, TikTokers, streamers, and influencers fall into this group.

What the report found 📊

$80 billionin unpaid taxes each year from income sole proprietors don't report
16%of the total yearly U.S. tax gap of about $496 billion
27.8 millionsole proprietors filed returns in 2019, about 18% of all taxpayers

The report says most of this is not about people trying to cheat. It points to three big reasons:

  • No taxes taken out. A regular job takes taxes out of each paycheck. Creators get paid in full, so they have to save and pay on their own.
  • Hard-to-read rules. Experts told the GAO that tax guidance for the self-employed is too technical.
  • Confusion about app and platform income. Some people don't know that money from online platforms is taxable, even when they don't get a tax form for it.

The GAO asked the IRS to do more to teach self-employed people about their taxes, and it asked Congress to let workers choose to have taxes taken out of their payments. As of early 2026, the GAO still listed all of these ideas as open.

"Most creators I meet aren't hiding anything. They just never had anyone explain the rules in plain words. A brand deal, a TikTok payout, free products you got for a video: it's all income. Once creators know that, and they learn which costs they can write off, taxes stop being scary."— Ruben Perez, Tax Advisor | AFSP, Creator Tax Pro

5 simple steps for creators before April 15 ✅

  1. Count every income stream. AdSense, brand deals, affiliate links, tips, subscriptions, merch, and gifted products all count. You owe tax on them even if you never get a Form 1099.
  2. Track your write-offs too. Reporting all your income doesn't mean paying more than you should. Gear, software, editors, and a home studio can lower what you owe. See our guide: What can content creators write off?
  3. Need more time? Ask for it. If your return won't be ready by April 15, 2027, you can file Form 4868 for an extension. It gives you more time to file, but not more time to pay. Any tax you owe is still due April 15.
  4. Fix past mistakes early. If you left income off an old return, an amended return (Form 1040-X) is usually much better than waiting for an IRS letter.
  5. Don't wait until April to pay. Put money aside from each payment and make quarterly estimated tax payments. Your last 2026 payment is due January 15, 2027.
